# Multiple Myeloma: Your Guide to Kidney Health

Kidney complications are common in Multiple Myeloma (MM), affecting up to 50% of patients. As a primary caretaker, understanding these challenges is vital for your loved one's well-being.

### Why MM Harms Kidneys

The main cause of kidney damage is the overproduction of abnormal proteins (light chains or Bence-Jones proteins) by myeloma cells. These proteins clog the kidney's filters, leading to **myeloma kidney**. Other factors include:

*   **High Calcium (Hypercalcemia):** Watch for increased thirst, frequent urination, confusion, constipation, or muscle weakness. Hydration and medications like bisphosphonates help.
*   **Amyloidosis:** Abnormal protein deposits can also damage kidneys, potentially influencing treatment choices.
*   **Medications:** Some treatments can affect kidney function.

Early, aggressive management is key. This means strict adherence to treatment, monitoring fluid intake/output, and reporting any changes in urination or swelling immediately. Ask your team: "What are the specific goals for kidney recovery?"

### What to Monitor: Tests & Signs

Regular kidney function tests are crucial, often checked weekly or bi-weekly during active treatment. Ask for lab printouts to track trends:

*   **Serum Creatinine:** Elevated levels (>1.2 mg/dL) indicate reduced function.
*   **eGFR:** A lower number (<60 mL/min/1.73m²) means impaired filtering.
*   **UPEP/IFE:** Detects kidney-damaging proteins.

**Home Monitoring:** Watch for swelling in legs/ankles, decreased urine output, increased fatigue, or changes in mental clarity. Report these immediately.

**Dietary Advice:** Discuss with the care team about potential restrictions or recommendations for sodium, potassium, and protein intake, which are crucial for kidney health.

### Treatments & Supportive Care

The focus is on rapidly reducing harmful light chains and supporting kidney function.

**Rapid Myeloma Control:**
*   **Bortezomib (Velcade®):** FDA-approved, given subcutaneously or intravenously. Monitor for peripheral neuropathy (tingling/numbness), fatigue, GI upset; report promptly.
*   **Carfilzomib (Kyprolis®):** FDA-approved, given intravenously. Watch for cardiovascular issues (shortness of breath, chest pain, swelling), hypertension (monitor blood pressure), and fatigue.
*   **Daratumumab (Darzalex®):** FDA-approved, primarily IV (subcutaneous option available). Pre-medication helps manage infusion reactions (chills, fever, rash) and fatigue.
*   **Isatuximab (Sarclisa®):** FDA-approved, given intravenously. Similar infusion reactions and fatigue as daratumumab.

**Supportive Care:**
*   **Plasmapheresis (Plasma Exchange):** In critical scenarios (rapidly worsening kidney function with very high light chains), this hospital procedure removes light chains. Expect fatigue; monitor for bleeding at access sites.
*   **Dialysis:** For severe kidney failure, hemodialysis (at a clinic) or peritoneal dialysis (at home) may be needed. Myeloma treatment often continues, with medication adjustments. Many patients maintain a good quality of life.

### Clinical Trials

Clinical trials offer access to cutting-edge treatments. Ask your medical team about trials, eligibility (especially regarding kidney function), and how participation might impact daily care. Resources like ClinicalTrials.gov can help you explore options.

By staying informed and actively engaging with the healthcare team, you play a vital role in your loved one's health and well-being.
