# Taking Control After a Chronic Migraine Diagnosis

A chronic migraine diagnosis can feel overwhelming, but taking immediate, structured steps empowers you to actively participate in your care and regain control. Focus on assembling your care team and meticulously tracking your migraine patterns.

## Finding Your Headache Specialist

While your primary care physician is crucial, chronic migraine often requires specialized care. Seek a **neurologist with a subspecialty in headache medicine**, ideally board-certified by the United Council for Neurologic Subspecialties (UCNS). These specialists offer in-depth knowledge and access to advanced treatments.

To find one, ask your primary care physician for a specific referral or use resources like the American Headache Society's website (ahsnet.org). Prioritize specialists who:

*   **Focus on Headache Medicine:** Inquire about their experience with chronic migraine patients.
*   **Are Affiliated with Headache Centers:** These often offer multidisciplinary support, including physical therapists and pain psychologists.
*   **Utilize Advanced Diagnostics:** While not for diagnosing migraine, these are crucial for ruling out other conditions with similar symptoms, ensuring an accurate diagnosis.
*   **Discuss Latest Treatments:** Inquire about newer preventive options:
    *   **CGRP Inhibitors:** These FDA-approved monoclonal antibodies (Aimovig, Ajovy, Emgality, Vyepti) target CGRP or its receptor to prevent migraines. Administered via self-injection or IV infusion, they are often considered when other preventives haven't worked. Common side effects include injection site reactions or constipation.
    *   **Botulinum Toxin Type A (Botox):** Also FDA-approved for chronic migraine prevention, Botox works by blocking pain signals. It involves multiple injections around the head and neck, typically for those with 15 or more headache days monthly who haven't responded to other preventives. Side effects can include neck pain, headache, or muscle weakness.
    *   If standard treatments aren't effective, ask about clinical trials through your specialist's office or websites like ClinicalTrials.gov.

## The Power of Your Migraine Diary

Starting a comprehensive migraine diary immediately is the most actionable and beneficial step. It's a critical diagnostic and treatment tool, providing invaluable data for your specialist.

**What to include (daily):**

*   **Date, Time, Duration:** Pinpoint attacks.
*   **Pain Severity:** Use a consistent 0-10 scale.
*   **Symptoms:** Note aura, nausea, light/sound sensitivity, fatigue, neck pain, mood changes.
*   **Potential Triggers:** Food, weather, stress, sleep, hormones, caffeine, smells. Be specific.
*   **Medications:** Acute and preventive, dosage, effectiveness, and side effects.
*   **Daily Activities & Stressors:** Brief notes on your day.
*   **Menstrual Cycle:** Crucial for women.

**Why it's crucial:**

*   **Accurate Diagnosis:** Helps confirm chronic migraine (15+ headache days/month for 3+ months, with 8+ being migraine-like).
*   **Trigger Identification:** Reveals patterns for avoidance strategies.
*   **Treatment Efficacy:** Provides objective data on how well medications work, guiding adjustments.
*   **Preventing Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Vital for tracking acute medication use to avoid this common complication.

Apps like Migraine Buddy or N-1 Headache can simplify diary keeping. Consistent, detailed tracking empowers both you and your specialist for better migraine management.
