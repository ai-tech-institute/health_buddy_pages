# Chronic Migraine: Advanced Preventive Therapies

For those with chronic migraine (15+ headache days/month, 8+ with migraine features), advanced preventive therapies offer significant hope beyond traditional oral medications.

## CGRP Inhibitors: Targeted Relief

**Mechanism:** These drugs block calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) or its receptor, preventing migraine pain signals.
*   **Erenumab (Aimovig):** FDA 2018, subcutaneous monthly.
*   **Fremanezumab (Ajovy):** FDA 2018, subcutaneous monthly or quarterly.
*   **Galcanezumab (Emgality):** FDA 2018, subcutaneous monthly.
*   **Eptinezumab (Vyepti):** FDA 2020, intravenous quarterly.

**Efficacy & Candidacy:** Often reduce monthly migraine days (MMDs) by 4-7, with many achieving a 50%+ reduction. Considered after failing two+ oral preventives.
**Side Effects:** Common: Constipation (erenumab), injection site reactions (cold pack helps). Rare but serious: Hypersensitivity, hypertension, cardiovascular concerns. Discuss any new symptoms with your neurologist.
**Patient Tracking:** Maintain a detailed headache diary (frequency, severity, symptoms, side effects). Ask your neurologist about monitoring progress and treatment adjustment criteria.

## OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox) Injections

**Mechanism:** Botox blocks pain-signaling neurotransmitters at nerve endings, modulating pain pathways.
**Efficacy & Candidacy:** Reduces MMDs by 7-9 days on average. FDA-approved for chronic migraine only. Improvement often seen after 2-3 rounds.
**Procedure:** 31-39 small injections into specific head/neck muscles every 12 weeks, taking 10-15 minutes. Mild, temporary discomfort is common.
**Contraindications:** Not recommended during pregnancy, breastfeeding, or with certain neuromuscular disorders (e.g., Myasthenia Gravis).

## Neuromodulation Devices: Drug-Free Options

These non-invasive devices use electrical or magnetic pulses to modulate nerve activity.
*   **sTMS (e.g., SpringTMS):** Magnetic pulses to interrupt cortical spreading depression.
*   **sTENS (e.g., Cefaly):** Electrical impulses to trigeminal nerve branches. FDA cleared for acute (2014) and preventive (2017) migraine. Studies show ~2-3 MMD reduction for prevention.
*   **nVNS (e.g., gammaCore Sapphire):** Stimulates the vagus nerve. FDA cleared for acute/preventive migraine and episodic cluster headache (2017/2018). Shows ~2-3 MMD reduction.

**Efficacy & Candidacy:** Reduce migraine frequency/intensity. Suitable for those seeking drug-free options, with contraindications to medication, or as add-on therapy. Consistent, correct use for several months is crucial for assessing benefit.
**Side Effects:** Skin irritation, tingling, mild pain, fatigue, jaw pain (device-specific).
**Acquisition:** Most require a prescription.

## Cost & Coverage

These advanced therapies are often expensive specialty treatments. Insurance coverage varies widely, typically requiring pre-authorization. Patient assistance programs may be available. Discuss financial aspects with your neurologist and insurance provider.

Discussing these options with your neurologist is key to determining the most suitable treatment plan to improve your quality of life.
